Titanium

Named for the Titans

Titanium is the ninth most common element in the Earth's crust, and for 150 years almost nobody could get it out. This is how it went from black sand to a card.

Keep scrolling →

1791

William Gregor, a Cornish clergyman, finds an unknown metal in the black sand of a stream near Manaccan.

Ilmenite sand, the ore Gregor studied. Guangdong Museum.

1795

Martin Heinrich Klaproth finds it again in rutile and names it after the Titans of Greek myth.

Klaproth, engraved by Ambroise Tardieu.

1925

Van Arkel and de Boer grow the first truly pure titanium, crystal by crystal, from titanium iodide on a glowing wire.

A titanium crystal bar made by the iodide process.

1940

William Kroll reduces it with magnesium. His process, and its grey sponge, still makes almost all the titanium in the world.

Titanium sponge from the Kroll process.

1964

The SR-71 Blackbird flies for the first time. Its airframe is mostly titanium, because at Mach 3 its skin runs hotter than 260 °C.

NASA's SR-71B over the Sierra Nevada, 1994.

Four parts, one card

A titanium face and back, a carbon core that carries the contactless antenna, and a gold-plated chip, bonded under heat and pressure.

  • Titanium face bead-blasted to a satin grain
  • Carbon core lets the antenna reach the reader
  • Gold-plated chip set flush with the metal
  • Titanium back laser-marked with your number

An anodised edge

Pass a current through titanium and it grows a film of oxide a few hundred nanometres thick. Light reflecting inside that film turns the metal blue, gold or violet.

No dye and no paint. The colour on the edge of every card comes from light alone.

Questions

Does contactless work through titanium?

Yes. The antenna sits in the carbon core and the metal is cut around it, so the card pays by tap at any contactless terminal.

Will the edge colour wear off?

The colour is the oxide itself, not a coating on top of it. Years of pockets and wallets soften it slightly; it never flakes.

Can I change the finish later?

Once a year at no cost. Send the old card back in the sleeve we include and we recycle its titanium.

What happens if I lose it?

Freeze it in the app straight away. A replacement reaches you within two days, and your virtual numbers keep working in the meantime.

Is Obverse a real card?

Not yet. This page is a design concept. Nothing on it is an offer of credit, and no card will be issued from it.
